# Human-readable questionnaire outline

The survey was self-administered in Portuguese or English and estimated to take approximately ten minutes. Participants were asked to imagine a city encounter in which autonomous vehicles communicate with pedestrians. The complete localized wording, answer labels, display logic, and HTML references are preserved in `survey.lss` and `survey.xml`.

## 1. Participant information

- Age group
- Gender
- Profession (collected in the original survey; not released)
- Nine Affinity for Technology Interaction items, rated on a six-point agreement scale

## 2. AR interfaces received from the vehicle

Each interface was rated for clarity on a seven-point scale and all six were then ranked from best to worst:

- Directional Arrow (survey display 1; manuscript I3)
- Vehicle Approaching (survey display 2; manuscript I2)
- Red Traffic Light (survey display 3; manuscript I5)
- Projected Crosswalk (survey display 4; manuscript I6)
- Yielding to Cross (survey display 5; manuscript I1)
- Green Traffic Light (survey display 6; manuscript I4)

An optional open-ended prompt asked for improvements or missing interactions.

## 3. Voice commands sent to the vehicle

Each command was rated for applicability on a seven-point scale and all five were ranked:

- Stop (C1), example: “Car, stop”
- Proceed (C2), example: “Car, go ahead”
- Avoid Obstacle (C3), example: “Car, go around that”
- Slow Down (C4), example: “Car, slow down”
- Wait for Pedestrians (C5), example: “Car, wait for more people”

An optional open-ended prompt asked for improvements or missing commands.

## 4. Gestures sent to the vehicle

Each looping gesture was rated for applicability on a seven-point scale and all nine were ranked:

- Slow Down (survey display 1; manuscript G7)
- Wait for Pedestrians (survey display 2; manuscript G9)
- T-Pose (survey display 3; manuscript G5)
- Proceed (survey display 4; manuscript G6)
- Avoid Obstacle (survey display 5; manuscript G1)
- Follow Me (survey display 6; manuscript G3)
- Stop (survey display 7; manuscript G8)
- Emergency Stop (survey display 8; manuscript G2)
- Go Back (survey display 9; manuscript G4)

An optional open-ended prompt asked for improvements or missing gestures.

## 5. Voice–gesture pairing

Participants selected one displayed gesture to accompany each of three commands: Stop, Proceed, and Slow Down. The matching screen used a different gesture order from the preceding rating/ranking screen. The preparation script maps both orderings to the manuscript G1–G9 identifiers.

## Archived media

The original audio clips, looping GIFs, and overview images are archived under `../stimuli/original_media/`. `MISSING_MEDIA.md` records the recovered filenames and distinguishes evaluated stimuli from two unrecovered theme-only assets.
